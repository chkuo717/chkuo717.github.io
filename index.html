Chin-Hsing Kuo · Mechanisms & Robotics

Mechanisms that carry themselves

I design mechanisms and robots that manage mechanical energy well: machines whose weight is carried by springs or magnets rather than by motors, linkages that move exactly as intended, and devices for surgery and rehabilitation.

Senior Lecturer in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Wollongong, Australia. Fellow of ASME. Associate Editor of the ASME Journal of Mechanical Design and Mechanism and Machine Theory.

Magnetic balancing

The same idea, without contact

Springs wear, rub and need adjusting. Magnets can do the same job without touching anything. At Wollongong my group designs arrangements of permanent magnets that hold up a load along a long stroke, float it in mid-air, or, like the ring here, balance an arm and can be re-set for a different load.

Research themes

Gravity balancing

Springs and gears arranged so that a mechanism's weight does no work at any posture.

Magnetic mechanisms

Permanent-magnet arrays and Halbach cylinders as contact-free springs and gravity compensators.

Parallel & decoupled mechanisms

Parallel manipulators whose motions are separated by design, not by control.

Reconfigurable mechanisms

Mechanisms that change their own topology, and the theory that describes them.

Medical & rehabilitation devices

Remote-centre-of-motion mechanisms for surgery, balancers for therapy.

Mechanism theory & teaching

Relative poles, structural analysis, and tools for learning kinematics by drawing.
