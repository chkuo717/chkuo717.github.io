A laparoscope holder that places, then aims

A holder for the camera in keyhole surgery has two jobs that should not mix: put the pivot point exactly at the incision, then swing and push the scope about that point. This design gives each job its own mechanism, and balances both with springs so the holder stays wherever it is left.

What you are seeing

The positioning arm is two parallelograms in series on a base pivot. Whatever the arm does, the pseudo-base link between the loops and the end-effector link at the tip stay upright, so a point fixed to the end-effector link, the remote centre of motion, is carried around without rotating. The surgeon places it at the incision. The orientating mechanism, mounted on the end-effector link, is a four-degree-of-freedom parallel mechanism of two five-bar chains whose output turns and slides the laparoscope about that point: pan, tilt, spin and insertion, with the incision point never moving. Position and orientation are controlled by different mechanisms, which is what the title calls decoupled manipulation.

The idea

Passive holders must stay put without brakes, which means the mechanism must be statically balanced in every configuration. Because the arm is made of parallelograms whose vertical links keep their orientation, each loop can be balanced by a single zero-free-length spring between its vertical link and its swinging link, with k h l equal to the moment of the weight the loop carries; the graph shows the two gravitational and spring energies adding to a flat line. The orientating mechanism is balanced too, by springs in its five-bars, so the whole holder is passive: no motors, no locks, and it can be moved by hand and released anywhere in its workspace.

In the paper

The paper derives the kinematics and the balancing conditions for both mechanisms, checks them numerically and in commercial software, evaluates the workspaces from a CAD model, and reports a prototype that was built and tested by hand. The remaining imbalance, from the springs' practical implementation, is small and quantified.

Dimensions and masses in the animation are illustrative; the structure, the two phases and the spring placement follow the paper's Figs. 2, 3 and 11.

The paper. Kuo, C.-H. and Lai, S.-J., "Design of a novel statically balanced mechanism for laparoscope holders with decoupled positioning and orientating manipulation," ASME Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, 2016. doi:10.1115/1.4029789
