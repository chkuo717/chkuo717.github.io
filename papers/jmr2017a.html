A balancer that re-sets itself to the load

A spring balancer is tuned to one weight. Put a different weight on this one and it moves its own spring attachments until it is balanced again, with no motor, sensor or hand.

What you are seeing

The arm is a parallelogram, so the platform on top stays level. One spring balances everything: it runs from slider A, which moves up and down a rail through the pivot, to slider B, which moves along the arm. With both sliders the same distance s from the pivot, the arm is balanced when k s² equals the moment of the weight, MgL. A heavier load therefore needs the sliders further out, a lighter one closer in.

The platform is a self-regulator: the payload rests on a weighing mechanism, and two cables run from it, one to each slider. When a different weight is placed on the platform the regulator deflects by a matching amount, the cables pay out or take in by the same length, and both sliders settle where k s² = MgL again. Nothing is measured electronically and nothing is powered.

The idea

Every spring balancer obeys one equation, k a b = mgL, where a and b are the distances of the two spring ends from the pivot. To cope with a changing load you can change the stiffness, change the arm, or change a and b. Moving the spring ends is the easiest mechanically, but doing it while the arm is away from upright costs energy, because the spring is stretched. The paper's answer is to let the adjustment happen only in the upright position, where the spring is slack and the sliders move for free, and to make both ends move together so that one weighing motion sets both. That is why the animation brings the arm home before it re-sets.

In the paper

The paper sets out the regulator mechanism and its cable routing, implements the zero-free-length spring with a compression spring and a third slider, derives the slider positions with the masses of the links and platform included, and builds a prototype that was tested with payloads of 1 kg and 2 kg. The prototype is pictured on the Gravity balancing page.

The animation keeps the paper's geometry and balancing condition but draws the regulator and cables schematically; see the paper for the mechanism inside the platform.

The paper. Chu, Y.-L. and Kuo, C.-H., "A single-degree-of-freedom self-regulated gravity balancer for adjustable payload," ASME Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, 2017. doi:10.1115/1.4035561
