A leg support that balances hip or knee

Some patients can move a leg only if someone else carries its weight, and the therapist must also keep the exercise to one joint at a time. This mechanism does both: it holds the leg’s weight at every angle, and it can be switched between knee-only and hip-only motion.

What you are seeing

The patient sits with the thigh on link 3 and the calf on link 4. The hip joint B is a pivot on the ground frame; the knee A is at the other end of the thigh link. Link 2 joins the base to a roller that runs in a slot in link 3, and links 4, 5 and 6 form a parallelogram with link 3. For knee-only exercise the linkage's constraints hold link 3 and the thigh fixed, and the calf swings about A: spring k2, between auxiliary link 9 and link 6, is a zero-free-length spring balancing a pendulum, and its stiffness is set so that k2h2l2 equals the moment of calf and foot. For hip-only exercise the knee is held instead; the thigh swings about B and the calf, carried by the parallelogram, translates without turning, so its weight acts like a mass at the knee. Spring k1, between link 3 and a bracket on the ground, balances the whole. In each mode the other spring keeps a constant length, so its stored energy does not change and it does not disturb the balance.

The idea

Reconfigurable mechanisms change their topology to do different jobs; gravity balancers remove the weight from a motion. The paper puts the two together for the first time in a rehabilitation device: one linkage, two working configurations selected by which joint is locked, and a spring installation chosen so that each configuration is balanced on its own, with the constant-potential-energy method applied to both. The design is sized from anthropometric data and verified in multibody simulation before building.

In the paper

A prototype was built in aluminium with linear springs arranged to act as zero-free-length springs through wire and pulley, and tested on a healthy subject with surface electromyography: with the balancer on, the muscle activity needed to move the leg in either mode fell markedly. The prototype is pictured on the Medical devices page.

Link masses are neglected in the animation, and the spring attachment distances are illustrative; the paper's Tables 1 to 3 give the full data and its computed stiffnesses, 5.69 and 1.50 N/mm.

The paper. Tseng, T.-Y., Lin, Y.-J., Hsu, W.-C., Lin, L.-F. and Kuo, C.-H., "A novel reconfigurable gravity balancer for lower-limb rehabilitation with switchable hip/knee-only exercise," ASME Journal of Mechanisms and Robotics, 2017. doi:10.1115/1.4036218
