Research · Parallel & decoupled mechanisms · Animation

One leg decides the motion, the others drive it

Designing a parallel manipulator usually means choosing all the legs together so that their constraints add up to the motion wanted. Here one leg does all the constraining, built like a serial robot with exactly the right freedoms, and the other legs only push.

What you are seeing

The bar rising from the slider is the motion constraint generator: a serial chain of one prismatic and one revolute joint, so the platform it carries can translate along the rail and turn, and do nothing else. The two legs to the left and right each have three revolute joints, the full freedom of a planar body, so they constrain nothing; their base joints are the motors. With both in place the mechanism has exactly two degrees of freedom and is non-overconstrained. Untick the constraint leg and the same two motors face three freedoms: the platform's position is no longer determined.

The idea

The usual synthesis of a parallel manipulator starts from the end-effector's motion type and asks for a set of identical legs whose constraints together produce it, which is a hard combinatorial problem and often yields overconstrained designs. The paper turns it round. Build the required motion as a serial chain, the constraint leg; then add auxiliary legs that have as many freedoms as the space allows (six in space, three in the plane), so they add no constraint, and put the actuators on them. Because the general mobility criterion is obeyed at every step, every manipulator that comes out is non-overconstrained, and the constraint leg can be enumerated from the well-known serial chains. The procedure also suggests decoupled designs, since the auxiliary legs can be placed to act on one freedom each.

In the paper

The paper gives the procedure as a flow chart and runs it for two-, three-, four- and five-degree-of-freedom cases: parallel wrists, 1R2T and 3R1T manipulators (including remote-centre-of-motion ones), and 3R2T manipulators, with the feasible leg structures tabulated. It is the method used for the surgical RCM manipulator.

The paper's examples are spatial; the plane keeps the idea visible. Leg lengths and the platform motion are illustrative.

The paper. Kuo, C.-H. and Dai, J. S., "Task-oriented structure synthesis of a class of parallel manipulators using motion constraint generator," Mechanism and Machine Theory, 2013. doi:10.1016/j.mechmachtheory.2013.08.010
