Research · Reconfigurable mechanisms · Animation

A cube of eight cubes that folds into other shapes

Eight small cubes hinged in a loop make a puzzle that folds from a cube into a slab and back into a cube of a different arrangement. As a mechanism it is a single loop of eight revolute joints whose mobility changes as hinges line up.

What you are seeing

The eight sub-cubes are connected in a closed chain by eight hinges, each along an edge shared by two neighbours. In the cube arrangement, two of the hinges lie on one line, so the half of the loop on one side of that line can swing as a rigid body: the cube opens into a flat slab of one by two by four. In the slab another pair of hinges is coaxial, and the far half folds under to make a cube again, but with the sub-cubes in a different arrangement. The real puzzle has eight such configurations, with pictures on its faces that appear one at a time; the paper's Fig. 1 shows them.

The idea

As a mechanism this is an 8R single-loop spatial linkage, and an unusual one: its topology is characterised not only by the relative orientations of the joint axes, as in most reconfigurable mechanisms, but also by the distances between them, since hinges on the same line (distance zero) behave differently from hinges that are merely parallel. The paper names these two properties orientation isomorphism and distance isomorphism, and shows that both are needed to say whether two configurations are the same. With screw theory it computes the mobility in each configuration and gives a unified procedure for identifying configuration isomorphism. Two examples make the point: two configurations that look quite different are shown to be identical, and two that look alike are shown to be distinct.

In the paper

The paper gives the schematic drawings and equivalent linkages of all eight configurations, the mobility analysis, the isomorphism criteria and the worked examples. The animation shows only the folds between a cube and a slab; the full set of configurations and their relationships are in the paper.

The hinge arrangement in the animation is a consistent loop that produces the cube-slab-cube sequence; the paper's exact hinge labelling is in its Fig. 4.

The paper. Kuo, C.-H. and Su, J.-W., "Configuration analysis of a class of reconfigurable cube mechanisms: mobility and configuration isomorphism," Mechanism and Machine Theory, 2017. doi:10.1016/j.mechmachtheory.2015.08.006
