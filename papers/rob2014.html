A wrist whose three motors do not interfere

A platform turns on a ball joint. Three motors on the three base axes each swing a plane; the platform has one point on each plane. Turn one motor and only its plane moves: the other two readings stay exactly where they were.

What you are seeing

The platform (the triangle) is held at its centre by a spherical joint, so it can only rotate. Three legs connect it to the base; the first joint of each leg is a motor on the x-, y- or z-axis, and the rest of the leg, a universal joint and three sliding joints, imposes no constraint except one: the platform point it holds must stay in the plane that contains the base axis and turns with the motor. The point held by the x-leg lies on the platform's v-axis, so the x-motor's angle is exactly the projective angle of v on the yz-plane; and so on round the cycle. Three plane angles, three orientation parameters, one to one.

The idea

The original Agile Eye is a three-degree-of-freedom spherical wrist with legs of three revolute joints whose axes all meet at the centre; it is fast and elegant but overconstrained, so it works only if the axes meet exactly. This variant replaces each leg by a chain that adds no constraint beyond the plane, and lets a single spherical joint do the constraining. The result is kinematically equivalent to the Agile Eye and non-overconstrained, so it tolerates manufacturing error, and its inverse kinematics is the identity: the motor angles are the projective angles of the platform. The paper's direct kinematics finds the orientation from the three plane angles by a geometric argument, which is what the Newton solve in the animation reproduces.

In the paper

The paper sets out the structure, proves the decoupling with screw theory, solves the inverse and direct kinematics, and examines the singularities and the workspace. The structural graph and the leg geometry are given in detail. It belongs with the general theory of decoupled projective motion.

Legs are drawn as their essential constraint, the turning plane with a slider on it; joint hardware is omitted. The platform points are at equal distance from the centre.

The paper. Kuo, C.-H., Dai, J. S. and Legnani, G., "A non-overconstrained variant of the Agile Eye with a special decoupled kinematics," Robotica, 2014. doi:10.1017/S0263574713001100
