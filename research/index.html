Research

Mechanical intelligence

My research is in robotics and mechanical design, organised around mechanical intelligence: the capacity of mechanisms and robots to adapt and to perform through their inherent mechanics, with minimal added sensing, computation, control and actuation. An arm that holds any posture because its springs cancel its weight is a simple example. The intelligence lies in the geometry and the springs, not in a controller.

The work runs from theory to devices. On the theoretical side it covers the structure and synthesis of mechanisms, including mechanisms with variable topologies, and the foundations of static balancing. On the technical side it develops machine elements that store and transform mechanical energy, from spring and counterweight balancers to contactless permanent-magnet springs, gravity compensators and levitators. These are applied to robotic manipulators, robotic hands and grippers, surgical and rehabilitation devices, consumer products and energy harvesting; four of the designs are granted US patents, and two have been transferred to industry. My long-term aim is robots and machines that interact with their physical environment effectively, efficiently and safely through their mechanical design.

In 2026 I founded the Symposium on Mechanical Intelligence in Mechanisms and Robotics at the ASME Mechanisms and Robotics Conference. Its first edition is at IDETC/CIE 2027 in Philadelphia.

Mechanical intelligence and AI

Physical AI puts intelligence in the software; mechanical intelligence puts it in the body. Today’s physical AI, with perception, reasoning, planning and action trained on real and synthetic data, treats the robot’s body as given. My work designs that body so that part of the task is done by the mechanism itself. Gravity balancing removes a disturbance that a controller would otherwise have to learn, and magnetic mechanisms shape force–motion behaviour that would otherwise be synthesised in software. Robots built this way need smaller actuators and are safer in contact, and they should need less data to train and behave more predictably from simulation to reality, because their behaviour follows from geometry and inherent mechanics rather than from high-gain compensation.

The engagement also runs the other way. I have no prior work in artificial intelligence, and this is the direction I most want to open, with mechanism theory as its footing: the AI-augmented synthesis and discovery of mechanisms. A large language model formalises a design task and proposes candidate mechanisms; exact tools from mechanism theory (graph enumeration, isomorphism tests, kinematic and static simulation, computer algebra and screw theory) then check every candidate, so that each result the model returns can be verified. Gravity balancing is a natural first target, since whether a design is balanced can be decided exactly: its total potential energy is either constant or not. The exact tools come from my own field; the language models and the learning would be developed with collaborators.

Research themes

Each theme page introduces the question and lists the published work under it, with a few representative papers first. Every representative paper has an animated page that computes its figure from the paper’s own geometry, and every other published paper has a smaller animation of its idea.

Gravity balancing

Springs and gears arranged so that a mechanism's weight does no work at any posture: four-bar linkages, serial arms, Delta robots, reconfigurable balancers.

Magnetic mechanisms

Permanent-magnet arrays and Halbach cylinders as contact-free springs, long-stroke gravity compensators and passive levitators.

Surgical & rehabilitation robotics

Remote-centre-of-motion mechanisms for minimally invasive surgery and craniotomy, statically balanced instrument holders, a reconfigurable balancer for lower-limb therapy.

Parallel & decoupled manipulators

Structure synthesis of parallel manipulators whose motions are separated by geometry: decoupled projective motion, a non-overconstrained Agile Eye.

Reconfigurable mechanisms

Mechanisms with variable topology, their mobility and configuration singularity, cube mechanisms, reconfiguration principles.

Structural synthesis of mechanisms

Mechanisms whose topology changes in operation: their variable joints, representations, mobility and configuration singularity; and the computational identification of equivalent links and joints in kinematic chains.

Mechanisms for renewable energy

Linkages and springs, including contact-free magnetic springs, that harvest green energy from wind, waves and vibration, or store it against gravity.

Mechanism theory & teaching

Relative poles for linkage synthesis and force analysis, and interactive tools for learning kinematics.

Collaborations

Journal papers from collaborations outside the themes above.

  • Origami-inspired deployable spatial structures with terrain adaptability.
    Wang, Y., Wang, X., Qu, H., Hu, B., Kuo, C.-H. and Guo, S., Engineering Structures, 2025. doi
    Four bistable units derived from Waterbomb origami form a channel module between two panels. Snapping the units on one side from concave to convex tilts the module by about 6.78°, so a chain of modules arches, or bends into an S, to fit the terrain.
  • Curved loop strip soft gripper.
    Liu, W., Qu, H., Wang, H., Wang, X., Kuang, Y., Hu, B., Li, X., Liu, D., Kuo, C.-H. and Guo, S., International Journal of Mechanical Sciences, 2025. doi
    A loop strip is bonded at the middle into a figure-eight; rotating the middle about the long axis makes the two lobes fold down and wrap the object. Rotating the other way gives a second mode for heavier loads.
  • Design and control of a novel variable stiffness series elastic actuator.
    Sariyildiz, E., Mutlu, R., Roberts, J., Kuo, C.-H. and Ugurlu, B., IEEE/ASME Transactions on Mechatronics, 2023. doi
    The output's twist pushes the rollers against leaf springs; sliding the rollers changes the springs' working length, and with it the actuator's stiffness, from about 20 N·m/rad to about 1 kN·m/rad.
  • Compliance error compensation of a robot end-effector with joint stiffness uncertainties for milling: an analytical model.
    Nguyen, V. L., Kuo, C.-H. and Lin, P. T., Mechanism and Machine Theory, 2022. doi
    A Delta robot carried by a parked industrial arm mills along a path; joint compliance makes the cut deviate (dashed). Springs on the Delta's actuated arms, sized by the paper's formulas, reduce the deviation.
