Research · Theme

Mechanism theory & teaching

Much of kinematics can be done by drawing. The relative pole, the point about which one link appears to rotate relative to another, lets a linkage be synthesised and its forces analysed with a straightedge and a compass, or, today, with a few lines of interactive geometry.

This theme collects the work that is about method rather than a particular device: an interactive e-book on relative poles used in my teaching and a bilingual book on the antique mechanism models kept at National Cheng Kung University.

Representative work

Each paper below has its own page with a working animation of its mechanism. They are drawn in the style of the papers’ own figures.

  • Book Linkage Synthesis and Force Analysis by Using Relative Poles. animation
    Kuo, C.-H., Interactive e-book, GeoGebra, 2024. link
    Two positions of a body: the perpendicular bisectors of A1A2 and B1B2 meet at the relative pole P12, and the body turns about it. Fixed pivots taken anywhere on the same bisectors give a four-bar linkage that makes the same move.
