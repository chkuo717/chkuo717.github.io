Research · Earlier work

Structural synthesis of mechanisms

Earlier work. My first four journal papers, from my bachelor's research at Tamkang University and my master's research with Hong-Sen Yan at National Cheng Kung University. The line is no longer active; the page is kept as a record of the published work.

Structural synthesis designs a mechanism from its topology: which links it has, how they are joined, and which of them are really different. These papers worked on two cases the classical theory left open: mechanisms whose topology changes while they run, and the automatic sorting of a chain's links and joints into equivalent groups.

A push-button lock, a key ring or a walking machine changes its structure during one cycle of operation: a pin that slides in a slot becomes a revolute joint when it reaches the end, a slider in an L-shaped slot changes direction at the corner, two parts are fastened for a while and then let go. The first paper treats the joint itself, the variable kinematic joint: its states are written as a sequence of joint codes, drawn as a digraph whose vertices are the states and whose arcs are the changes, and stored as matrices. From the digraph follow the joint's topological properties, among them reversibility, continuity, the variation of its degrees of freedom, and when two variable joints are homomorphic. The second paper does the same for the whole mechanism, adding its motion states as a finite-state machine with state tables and state graphs, and defines topological and motion homomorphism to tell when two such mechanisms are structurally, and kinematically, the same.

The third paper counts the freedoms. Each stage of a mechanism with variable topologies is described by the screws of its joints at that stage, so that a change of topology is a change in the set of screw coordinates and the mobility follows from its rank; the configurations at which the topology changes are then read as stationary or uncertainty configurations. The fourth paper turns to ordinary kinematic chains and finds, by computation rather than inspection, which links and which joints are topologically equivalent, so that a synthesis that enumerates mechanisms does not produce the same one twice.

The master's thesis was also published as a book. The ideas carried on into my later work on reconfigurable mechanisms.

Representative work

Each paper below has its own page with a working animation of its mechanism. They are drawn in the style of the papers’ own figures.

  • Computational identification of link adjacency and joint incidence in kinematic chains and mechanisms. animation
    Kuo, C.-H. and Shih, J.-C., ASME Journal of Mechanical Design, 2008. doi
    The graph of Watt's chain, links as vertices and joints as edges. Links and joints with the same adjacency are sorted into classes by computation, shown by colour; fixing link 1 splits the classes.
  • On the mobility and configuration singularity of mechanisms with variable topologies. animation
    Kuo, C.-H. and Yan, H.-S., ASME Journal of Mechanical Design, 2007. doi
    In the push-button stopper lock the frame, slider, button and stopper change their joints from stage to stage: locking, unlockable, unlocking and password changeable. Counted with screw systems the mobility is 1, 2, 1, 2, where the Grübler criterion does not give it.

Also under this theme

  • Representations and identifications of structural and motion state characteristics of mechanisms with variable topologies.
    Yan, H.-S. and Kuo, C.-H., Transactions of the Canadian Society for Mechanical Engineering, 2006. doi
    A Geneva mechanism whose slider clamps and releases the wheel. The slider–wheel joint passes through fixed, sliding and separated states, which the paper represents with joint sequences and state graphs.
  • Topological representations and characteristics of variable kinematic joints.
    Yan, H.-S. and Kuo, C.-H., ASME Journal of Mechanical Design, 2006. doi
    A two-member key ring: one joint that slides, then slides and turns, then only turns. The paper writes such variable joints as joint codes and digraphs of their states.
  • Book Structural Characteristics of Mechanisms with Variable Topologies: Taking into Account the Configuration Singularity.
    Kuo, C.-H., VDM Verlag Dr. Müller, Saarbrücken, 2008. link
    A joint whose pin leaves an arc for a straight slot, turning about z and then sliding along x: the kind of variable joint whose configurations the book analyses.
