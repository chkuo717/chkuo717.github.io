Chin-Hsing Kuo · Robotics & Mechanical Design

Robots that carry themselves

I am a roboticist and mechanical designer working on mechanical intelligence: the capacity of mechanisms and robots to adapt and to perform through their inherent mechanics, with minimal added sensing, computation, control and actuation. Robot arms whose weight is carried by springs or magnets rather than by motors, surgical and rehabilitation devices that hold a load without fighting the hand that guides them, parallel robots whose motions are separated by their geometry, and mechanisms that turn irregular motion into useful energy are all examples. Physical AI puts intelligence in the software; mechanical intelligence puts it in the body.

Research themes

Gravity balancing

Springs and gears arranged so that a mechanism's weight does no work at any posture.

Magnetic mechanisms

Permanent-magnet arrays and Halbach cylinders as contact-free springs and gravity compensators.

Surgical & rehabilitation robotics

Remote-centre-of-motion mechanisms for surgery, balanced instrument holders and balancers for limb therapy.

Parallel & decoupled manipulators

Parallel robots whose motions are separated by their geometry rather than by control.

Reconfigurable mechanisms

Mechanisms that change their own structure as they move, and how to design them on purpose.

Structural synthesis of mechanisms

Designing a mechanism from its topology: representations, mobility and equivalent links and joints.

Mechanisms for renewable energy

Linkages and magnetic springs that harvest green energy from wind, waves and vibration, or store it against gravity.

Mechanism theory & teaching

Relative poles and tools for learning kinematics by drawing.
