Counting freedoms when the topology keeps changing

A push-button lock is a mechanism whose joints change type as you use it: a pin that could only slide can suddenly also turn. The ordinary formula for counting a mechanism’s freedoms fails on it; counting with screws does not.

What you are seeing

Four parts: the frame, a slider that runs through it, a button that runs through both, and a stopper on the button. Locked, the stopper sits in a groove in the slider and the slider cannot move; only the button can, one freedom. Press the button and the stopper clears the groove: the slider is free as well, two freedoms. Move the slider and the button is trapped in it, one freedom again. At a special slider position the stopper can be turned into another groove, which changes the password: a transitional stage with two freedoms. The screw-system count under the figure gives 1, 2, 1, 2; the planar Grübler count gives 1, 0, 1 and −1, which is wrong in two stages out of four.

The idea

In a mechanism with variable topology the joints do not have fixed types: a prismatic pair may become a cylindrical pair when a groove lines up, or a fixed pair when a stop is met. The paper represents each configuration by the set of screws of its joints at that instant, so that a change of topology is a change in the set of screw coordinates, and takes the mobility from the rank of that set. The same screw representation describes the two kinds of singular configuration through which topology changes happen, stationary configurations and uncertainty configurations, which is the basis for designing when and how a mechanism should switch.

In the paper

Two examples carry the mobility analysis, the push-button lock and a kinematotropic PSPS chain with changeable mobility; the configuration singularity section works through stationary and uncertainty configurations with a four-bar and other chains. The paper is one of the early theoretical treatments of mechanisms with variable topologies, from Kuo’s master’s research with Hong-Sen Yan at National Cheng Kung University.
