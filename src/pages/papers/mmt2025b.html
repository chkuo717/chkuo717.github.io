Balancing any four-bar linkage with two springs

A weight anywhere on the coupler of a four-bar linkage, with any link lengths, can be carried at every position by two springs on the ground. Here is the construction, with the numbers.

What you are seeing

A crank, a coupler and a rocker, with a weight m somewhere on the coupler. Two springs run from anchors on the ground to short arms fixed to the crank and to the rocker. As the crank turns, the weight rises and falls, the springs stretch and relax, and the red line in the graph, the total stored energy, stays flat. The linkage would rest wherever it is left.

The springs are zero-free-length springs, whose pull is proportional to their whole length; that is the standard idealisation in spring balancing, and the paper discusses how such springs are realised in practice.

The idea

The height of a point on the coupler is not a simple function of the crank angle, which is why balancing a four-bar linkage has been treated case by case. The paper shows that it need not be. Written in complex numbers, the position of any coupler point is a sum of two terms: one that turns with the crank and one that turns with the rocker, plus a constant. For gravity, then, the single weight on the coupler is exactly equivalent to one weight on the crank and one on the rocker, each at a fixed radius and offset that the formula gives. Each of those is a plain pendulum, and a pendulum is balanced by one zero-free-length spring between a ground anchor and a point on the link, with k h l = mgr. Two springs therefore balance the whole linkage, for any link lengths and any coupler point, with no approximation. The same decomposition extends to weights on all the links.

In the paper

The paper develops this for the general four-bar linkage with the masses of all four links, gives closed-form expressions for the spring parameters, treats the special cases, and shows the solutions numerically. It is a single-author paper and the source of the animation on the home page of this site, which shows the same linkage with the controls reduced to a single switch.

The dimensions here are illustrative; the spring anchors and arms are placed according to the paper's construction and the energies are computed at each position.
