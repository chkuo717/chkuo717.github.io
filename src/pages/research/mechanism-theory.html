Mechanism theory & teaching

Much of kinematics can be done by drawing. The relative pole, the point about which one link appears to rotate relative to another, lets a linkage be synthesised and its forces analysed with a straightedge and a compass, or, today, with a few lines of interactive geometry.

This theme collects the work that is about method rather than a particular device: an interactive e-book on relative poles used in my teaching, computational tools for identifying the structure of kinematic chains, and a bilingual book on the antique mechanism models kept at National Cheng Kung University.
