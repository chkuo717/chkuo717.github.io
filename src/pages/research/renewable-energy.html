Mechanisms for renewable energy

Wind, ocean waves, vibrating structures and moving machines all carry mechanical energy, but rarely in the form a generator wants: the motion is slow, unsteady and often back-and-forth, while generators work best with steady, fast rotation. The mechanism between the two decides how much of that energy becomes useful power.

This is where my group at Wollongong is now directing its work: linkages and springs, including contact-free magnetic springs, that turn irregular motion into a form a generator can use, and mechanisms that store energy by raising a weight and return it on demand.

The direction grows from two of the other themes. Gravity balancing is, at heart, an exchange of potential energy between a weight and a spring, and magnetic mechanisms supply springs that do not wear. Results will be listed here as they are published.
